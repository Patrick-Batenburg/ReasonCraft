# Reasons and objections

Arguments come in **cards**. A card is a rounded frame that holds one or more premises and points at what it is about: the claim, or a premise of another card.

- A **reason** card (green) supports what it points at. Its line ends in an arrowhead.
- An **objection** card (red) opposes what it points at. Its line ends in a short flat bar.

A good map looks at both sides. Objections are not a weakness of your map: answering them is what makes an argument convincing.

## Adding reasons and objections

- Select the claim or a premise and press **Tab** to add a reason, or **Shift+Tab** to add an objection.
- Or use Home > Reason and Home > Objection, the **+** button next to the selected box, or drag Reason or Objection from the Building panel onto a box.
- While you type in a box, Tab finishes it and adds a reason straight away, so you can keep writing.

## Changing your mind

Select a card (or one of its premises) and use Home > **Type** to turn a reason into an objection or back. Any strength rating it had is kept.

## What a card is about

A card under a premise is about that premise only: it says the premise is true (a reason) or false (an objection). A card can also be about another card as a whole: then it says that the step from the premises to their conclusion holds, or that it does not. See [Whole card or one premise](whole-card-vs-premise).

## Several reasons

Give each separate reason its own card. Two reasons that each support the claim on their own belong in two cards side by side. Two statements that only work together belong in one card as co-premises. See [Co-premises](co-premises).

## Answering an objection

You can put an objection under an objection. It answers the first one, and ReasonCraft shows it as a **rebuttal** (orange). See [Rebuttals](rebuttals).
