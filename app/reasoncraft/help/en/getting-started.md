# Getting started

ReasonCraft helps you think a question through by drawing it as a map. You put a **claim** at the top, then add the **reasons** that support it and the **objections** that oppose it, and back them up with **sources**. The map shows at a glance how your argument holds together, and the Text panel turns it into an outline and an essay.

## The window

- The **ribbon** at the top holds the commands, on the tabs File, Home, Insert, Evaluate, View, Export and Help. Insert gathers everything that adds to the map. When the window is too narrow for a tab, arrows at its ends scroll it (so does the mouse wheel).
- The **Building panel** on the left lists boxes, sources, preset comments and templates. Click a box, source or comment to add it at the selection, or drag it onto the map; click a template to start a new map from it.
- The **map** in the middle is where you build. Scroll with the mouse wheel (Shift+wheel sideways), or drag with the right or middle mouse button, or hold Space and drag. Ctrl+wheel zooms, as do the View tab and the slider in the status bar.
- The **Text panel** on the right has the tabs Outline, Essay, Check, Snippets and Help.
- The **status bar** at the bottom counts the boxes and the hints of the Check tab.

View > Building panel and View > Text panel show or hide the side panels.

## Your first map

- Click the empty box at the top and type your claim, for example "Our school should start an hour later". Typing on a selected box edits it; press Esc when you are done.
- With the claim selected, press Tab to add a reason. Type why the claim is true.
- Press Shift+Tab on the claim to add an objection: a reason against it.
- Press Ctrl+Enter on a premise to add a co-premise: a second statement that supports the claim only together with the first. See [Co-premises](co-premises).
- Use the arrow keys to move between boxes, and Enter or F2 to edit the selected box.
- Add a source under a premise with Ctrl+Shift+E, or pick a type from Home > Source.
- Save with Ctrl+S. Maps are saved as `.rcraft` files.

On a Mac, use Cmd where this help says Ctrl, and Option where it says Alt. The page [Keyboard shortcuts](keyboard) lists the keys of your computer.

You can also select a box and use the small **+** button next to it, or the Home tab: Reason, Objection, Co-premise, Source, Category and Note are all there.

## Items that are not connected yet

Anything you drop on empty canvas, or that is left over when you delete the box above it, goes to the area marked **Not connected**. It is saved with the map, but it is not part of the argument yet. Drag it onto a box to connect it, or select it and use Home > **Move to…** (Ctrl+Shift+K), then click where it belongs (or pick the place with the arrow keys and press Enter). Move to… moves connected items too, and Home > **Move to parking** takes one out of the map. Ctrl+Shift+P jumps between the map and this area.

## Undo, find and more

- Every change can be undone with Ctrl+Z and redone with Ctrl+Y.
- Ctrl+F finds text in boxes, sources and notes; Ctrl+H also replaces it.
- The **Search commands** box in the title bar (Alt+Q) finds any command by name.
- F6 moves the keyboard focus between the ribbon, the panels, the map and the status bar.
- F1 opens help about the part of the window you are working in.

## Example maps and templates

To see a finished map first, open the Help tab of the ribbon and click **Reasoning example** or **Grouping example** in the group **Example maps**. The reasoning example shows reasons, objections, co-premises, a rebuttal, sources and ratings; change it as much as you like, it is a copy. File > **New** has the same examples, plus templates to start from, such as an essay plan.
