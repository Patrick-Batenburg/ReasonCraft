# Rating your map

Building a map shows what the arguments are. Rating it shows what you think of them: which statements you believe, which reasons are strong, and which sources you trust. Rating is optional, and nothing changes until you start.

## Three kinds of rating

- **Claims and premises**: **Accept** (you believe it), **Unsure** (you doubt it) or **Reject** (you think it is false). The box gets a small mark: a check mark, a question mark or a cross.
- **Cards** (reasons, objections and rebuttals): **Strong**, **Weak** or **Nil**. This rates how well the premises support or oppose their target, if they are true. A strong card shows two dots and a label such as "strongly supports"; a weak card one dot. A nil card is greyed out and its label says "does not support".
- **Sources**: **Solid**, **Shaky** or **Nil**: how far you trust the source.

## How to rate

- Select a box, card or source and use the Evaluate tab: the groups **Claims and premises**, **Cards** and **Sources** hold the buttons.
- Or use the keys: **Alt+1** rates high (accept, strong, solid), **Alt+2** mid (unsure, weak, shaky), **Alt+3** low (reject, nil) and **Alt+0** clears the rating. On a Mac these are Option+Cmd+1, 2, 3 and 0.
- **Clear** > Selection removes the rating of the selected item; Clear > Map (Alt+Shift+0, or Option+Shift+Cmd+0 on a Mac) removes every rating in the map.

## What you see

Ratings tint the boxes and cards: the stronger the rating, the more colour and the thicker the border. Lines get thicker for strong cards and thinner for weak ones. On the Evaluate tab (and the View tab), **Show markers** turns the marks on boxes off and on, and **Evaluation shading** does the same for the tint.

## Rating well

Rate from the bottom up. First ask whether the sources are solid, then whether the premises are acceptable, and only then how strong each card is. A card with strong-looking premises that you reject cannot carry much weight.

The Check tab helps you here:

- Once you start rating, it reminds you of the items you have not rated yet.
- It points out ratings that disagree: a card rated strong while all its premises are rejected, a source rated solid under a rejected premise, or a card rated nil while its premises are accepted and nothing objects to it.

See [Checks](checks).

## Strongest first

The Essay tab orders its sections by your ratings: strong cards first, then unrated, weak and nil ones. See [Outline and essay](outline-and-essay).

## Comments

Teachers, classmates and colleagues can leave feedback on any box. Select a box and use Home > **Comment** (Ctrl+Shift+M), or pick one of the preset comments in the Building panel, such as "Needs more evidence." A comment can be marked Positive, Needs work or Problem, and a badge on the box shows how many comments it has. File > Settings sets the name shown on your comments.
