# Grouping maps

Not every map argues for something. A **grouping map** sorts a topic into categories: the topic goes at the top, the main categories under it, and smaller categories under those. Use one to collect ideas before you write, to plan the parts of a report, or to compare kinds of things.

## An example

Topic: "Ways to get around town"

- Public transport, with Buses and Trams under it
- Cycling
- Walking

## Building a grouping map

- Start a new map with File > New (Ctrl+N) and type the topic in the top box.
- With the topic selected, use Home > **Category**, or drag Category from the Building panel onto the topic.
- Select a category and add another category to put it underneath.
- Notes and comments can go on any category.

Categories are joined by neutral lines without arrowheads or bars: they mean "belongs to", not "supports" or "opposes".

## One kind per map

What you put under the top box first decides the kind of map:

- reasons or objections make it a reasoning map;
- categories make it a grouping map.

A map is one or the other. Make a separate map (and file) for each. Categories take no sources, reasons, objections or ratings.

## What works differently

- The Outline lists the categories in order, with numbers if you like. See [Outline and essay](outline-and-essay).
- A grouping map has no essay: the Essay tab says so, and Export > Essay is greyed out. The rating checks are for reasoning maps only too.
- The Check tab reports a main category with nothing under it (**Empty categories**).
- Exports and Argdown work as for any map. See [Exports and printing](exports-and-print).
