# Co-premises

Sometimes one statement is not enough to support a claim, but two statements together are. Those statements are **co-premises**: they sit side by side in one card, and the card supports (or opposes) its target only as a whole.

## An example

Claim: "Sam should wear a helmet."

- Premise: "Sam cycles to school every day."
- Co-premise: "People who cycle every day should wear a helmet."

The first premise alone does not say why Sam needs a helmet; the second alone does not mention Sam. Together they make the reason complete. If either one is false, the reason falls.

## Adding a co-premise

- Select a premise or its card and press **Ctrl+Enter** (Cmd+Enter on a Mac), or use Home > **Co-premise**.
- Or drag Co-premise from the Building panel onto a card.
- The new premise appears next to the others in the same card.

## Co-premise or a second reason?

Ask: does this statement support the claim on its own?

- If it does, it is a separate reason: give it its own card with Tab.
- If it only works together with another premise, it is a co-premise: add it to that card with Ctrl+Enter.

## Hidden co-premises

Many arguments leave a co-premise unsaid because it seems obvious. Writing it down shows what your argument really assumes, and lets others question it. The key-word check helps you find these missing co-premises: when words of the claim appear in none of a card's premises, a premise is probably missing. See [Checks](checks).

## Moving co-premises

Drag a premise between the other premises of its card to change their order, or select it and press Alt+Left or Alt+Right. In a map laid out from left to right the co-premises stack vertically, and Alt+Up and Alt+Down move them.
