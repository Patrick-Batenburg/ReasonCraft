# Whole card or one premise

A reason or objection can be aimed at two different things, and the difference matters.

## Aimed at one premise

A card under a premise is about that premise only. An objection there says: "this statement is not true". Its line runs to the bottom of that premise's box.

Example: the premise "Sam cycles to school every day" gets the objection "Sam takes the bus when it rains."

## Aimed at the whole card

A card can also be aimed at another card as a whole. Then it does not say that a premise is false. It is about the step from the premises to what they support:

- An objection to the whole card says: "even if these premises are true, they do not support that". It attacks the link.
- A reason for the whole card says: "these premises really do support that". It backs the link.

Its line runs to the edge of the card's frame, not to one of the premises.

Example: the reason "Many students in our class have a part-time job" supports the claim "Homework should be optional". An objection to the whole card could be: "Having a job does not change how much practice students need." The premise may well be true; the objection says that it does not lead to the claim.

## How to add one

- Select a card, or a premise in it, and press **Ctrl+Shift+R** for a reason for the whole card or **Ctrl+Shift+O** for an objection to the whole card.
- Or use the **+** button next to the selection: it offers "Reason for the whole card" and "Objection to the whole card".
- Or drag Reason or Objection from the Building panel onto the edge of a card's frame.

## Which one do I need?

Ask yourself what you doubt:

- "That is not true": aim at the premise.
- "That is true, but it does not prove the point": aim at the whole card.
