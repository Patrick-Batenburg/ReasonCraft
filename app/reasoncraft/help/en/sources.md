# Sources

A premise is only as good as what backs it up. A **source** is the evidence under a premise (or under the claim): where you got it from, or why you believe it. Sources have no border on the map: they show an icon, their type in bold and your description underneath, and hang under their premise by a thin grey line.

## Adding a source

- Select a premise and press **Ctrl+Shift+E** to add a source, then type its description.
- Or pick a type from Home > **Source**, from the **+** button next to the selection, or from the Sources section of the Building panel (click it, or drag it onto a premise).
- With nothing selected, a new source goes to Not connected.
- Change the type of a selected source later with Home > **Type**.

Write enough to find the source again: who, what, where and when. An empty source shows a hint of what to write, such as "Title, author, year" for a publication.

## The source types

- **Source**: any source, when no other type fits.
- **My own basis**: what you base this on yourself.
- **Assertion**: someone says so; note who and where.
- **By definition**: true because of what the words mean.
- **Case study**: one case looked at closely.
- **Common belief**: something many people believe.
- **Data**: a dataset, with its owner and year.
- **Event**: something that happened, with where and when.
- **Example**: an example that shows the point.
- **Expert opinion**: what an expert in the field says.
- **Law**: a law or rule, with its article.
- **Media**: a programme, video or podcast.
- **Personal experience**: something you went through yourself.
- **Publication**: a book, article or report.
- **Quote**: someone's exact words.
- **Statistic**: a figure, with where it comes from.
- **Web**: a web page, with the date you visited it.

## How reliable is it?

Not every source is equally strong. A statistic from a national office says more than one blog post; your own experience is a single case. Rate each source on the Evaluate tab: **Solid**, **Shaky** or **Nil**. See [Rating your map](evaluation).

## Links

Give a source a web link with Home > **Link**. The link is saved with the map and opens in your browser only when you click its link symbol or use Insert > **Open link** (Ctrl+Shift+L).

## Sources from snippets

When you collect text in the Snippets tab, you can tag each snippet with a source type, a citation and a link. Drag text from that snippet onto the map and the source comes along under the new premise. See [Snippets](snippets).
